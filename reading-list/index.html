Who's holding what now? Reading list

From the talk Who's holding what now? New tools, shifting burdens and learning to keep up, MoT London, Nov 2026.

Start with the recommended reads. The further reading is grouped so that pieces that disagree sit side by side. Read them together and make up your own mind.

Recommended reads

  1. Lee et al. (Microsoft Research & Carnegie Mellon), The Impact of Generative AI on Critical Thinking (CHI 2025)

    More confidence in the AI went with less critical thinking; more confidence in yourself went with more. A clear look at why how we engage matters.

    Paper (open access)

  2. BCG, When Everyone Uses AI, Companies Risk Losing Critical Skills (2026)

    Leaders on de-skilling, overreliance and reduced ownership. Most of its fixes are human ones: pairing, mentoring, AI-off practice.

    BCG

  3. Bedard, Kellerman et al. (BCG), When Using AI Leads to "Brain Fry" (HBR, 2026)

    Mental fatigue from using and overseeing AI tools. It also shows AI reducing burnout when it takes away repetitive work. What matters is where the freed time goes.

    BCG summary

    Leigh Rathbone's post on the same feeling: LinkedIn

  4. Ranganathan & Ye, AI Doesn't Reduce Work—It Intensifies It (HBR, 2026)

    Eight months inside one tech company: people took on more, filled their breaks and let work spill into evenings. Suggests an "AI practice" of pauses, sequencing and protected human time.

    HBR · Berkeley Haas summary

  5. Shen & Tamkin (Anthropic), How AI assistance affects coding skills (2026)

    A practical example: people who kept asking why and how kept their learning.

    Anthropic · Paper

  6. Kosmyna et al. (MIT Media Lab), Your Brain on ChatGPT (2025)

    A deep look at what happens in the brain when an LLM does the writing, and the idea of "cognitive debt". Small study, by its own admission. Read the conclusion at least.

    Paper

  7. Tanya Reilly, Being Glue

    What glue work is, why it matters and what it costs the people who do it.

    noidea.dog/glue

  8. DORA, 2025 State of AI-assisted Software Development

    AI as an amplifier. Practical frameworks and team archetypes for working out which practices fit your team.

    Report (PDF)

    MoT

Further reading, side by side

More output, more pressure

The gains are real. So is the pressure that comes with them.

  • Korn Ferry, Workforce 2026. 52% say AI has increased their workload, 63% say it made them more efficient, and 52% feel adequately trained to use it. Korn Ferry · Write-up

  • Dell'Acqua et al., The Cybernetic Teammate. At P&G, one person with AI matched a team without it, on short tasks. Read its own limitations section, and ask how you'd use those gains for your team, not just the bottom line. NBER

  • Niederhoffer, Hancock et al., AI-Generated "Workslop" Is Destroying Productivity (HBR, 2025). The other side: output that looks done and isn't, handed to someone else to fix. HBR

Learning with AI, or losing to it

Same tools, different results, depending on how they're used.

  • Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, Generative AI at Work (QJE, 2025). Big gains for novices in customer support, measured mostly as speed. NBER

  • Bastani et al., Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning (PNAS, 2025). Plain answers hurt later exam scores; an AI tutor that asked questions didn't. SSRN

  • Bjork & Bjork, Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way (2011). Desirable difficulties: why some kinds of hard build ability. Bjork Lab

Skill decay: the business case and the human one

  • Taylor, Shumsky & Siderius (Dartmouth Tuck), Use it or Slowly Lose it (2026). A business model of skill atrophy. The risk is highest when AI is right most of the time. Tuck

  • Budzyń et al., endoscopist deskilling (Lancet Gastroenterology & Hepatology, 2025). Unaided detection fell after AI came in. Small study. Paper · Expert reactions

  • Casner et al., The Retention of Manual Flying Skills in the Automated Cockpit (Human Factors, 2014). Pilots got here first. Paper

  • Parasuraman & Riley, Humans and Automation: Use, Misuse, Disuse, Abuse (1997). Older, but the fundamentals of calibrated trust still hold. Paper

Trust and ownership

  • Stack Overflow Developer Survey 2026. Most developers trust AI when they can verify it; very few trust it with big decisions. Survey · Summary

  • MIT Technology Review Insights / Infosys (2025). Psychological safety and AI success; one for leaders. Vendor-sponsored. Infosys

  • Gallup 2026. Engagement is higher where there's a clear AI plan and managers actively support its use. Gallup · Fortune

Juniors: who learns the garden?

Different sources, different stories. Look at what each one is measuring.

  • Brynjolfsson, Chandar & Chen, Canaries in the Coal Mine (2026 update). Fewer hires of young workers in AI-exposed jobs (US). Stanford

  • techUK, What's actually happening with entry-level and graduate jobs. The UK picture, with a business lean. techUK

  • Yu, Moon et al. (Seoul National University), Who Will Become the Next Senior? (2026). Losing the "opportunity to fail". TechXplore summary

  • IBM is tripling entry-level hiring Axios · AWS CEO: replacing juniors with AI is "one of the dumbest things I've ever heard" The Register

Where we used to help each other

  • del Rio-Chanona et al., Large language models reduce public knowledge sharing on online Q&A platforms (PNAS Nexus, 2024). Paper

  • Pragmatic Engineer, Stack Overflow is almost dead. The easier read on the same story. Blog

  • Babcock et al., Gender Differences in Accepting and Receiving Requests for Tasks with Low Promotability (AER, 2017). Who ends up doing the glue work. Paper

Tools from the talk

  • Gary Klein, Performing a Project Premortem (HBR, 2007). Decide before the tool lands. HBR

  • Angela Glover Blackwell, The Curb-Cut Effect (SSIR, 2017). Built for one group, useful to everyone. SSIR

  • TJ Nealy, How to tell if you're using AI too much (TEDxCharlotte). YouTube
